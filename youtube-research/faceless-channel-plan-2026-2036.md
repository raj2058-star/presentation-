# Faceless YouTube research and a 10-year channel plan (2026–2036)

Researched November 2026 with the `/yt-viral` skill. The evidence files are in this folder:
- `channel-baselines.csv`: the median, best video and average length for 46 faceless channels
- `outliers-3x.csv`: 112 videos that got at least 3× their own channel's median views

---

## 0. Read this first

- **Nobody can guarantee millions of views.** In this sample, only about a dozen of 46 established faceless
  channels have a *median* above 1M views per video. Most top channels took years to get there. This plan is
  built to make million-view videos *likely* and *repeatable*. It cannot make them certain.
- **"Copy" here means copying the structures that work, never the content.** YouTube's *inauthentic
  content* policy (renamed from "repetitious content" in July 2025) demonetizes mass-produced, templated
  videos: TTS narration over stock footage, AI slideshows with no original commentary, and listicle
  channels where only the topic changes. Sources describe faceless and AI-assisted channels being cut off
  for exactly this ([newmoneymatrix](https://newmoneymatrix.org/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-explained/),
  [nofacetube](https://nofacetube.com/guides/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy/),
  [aituber](https://aituber.app/blog/faceless-youtube-channels-demonetized-2026/)).
  Being faceless is fine. Being generic is not.
- **Some of the biggest outliers are borrowed brands.** "Restoring Burned FRANK from *Cars*" (304×), an
  AI *World Cup* movie (637×) and *Baki* tributes all ride someone else's brand, which is a copyright and
  trademark risk. They are excluded from the plan.

### How the data was collected

I read the public video listings (last 30 long-form uploads) of 46 faceless channels across
documentary, explainer, history, finance, horror, ambient and animation. I also ran YouTube searches for
2026's most-viewed long-form videos on 20+ topics. Every video is scored as a **multiple of its own
channel's median**, so the ranking reflects the idea rather than the channel's size.
The limits:
- View counts are lifetime counts, and YouTube rounds them.
- 30 videos is a snapshot of each channel.
- A few searches were rate-limited.
- The formula labels describe the *title wording*. They are not a claim about *why* a video worked.

## 2. The gap

I searched "2050 future", "the future of" and "what will happen 2030" for 2026:
- **No faceless channel clearly leads the future as a general topic.** The results are mostly brand ads, Mrwhosetheboss
  (who shows his face), and small channels (Cosmic Echo at a 22K median, Infinite Planet TV at 346K).
- **Demand is clearly there.** Maé has just 10 long-form videos, a **925K median**, and *Everything That Will Happen Before 2050* at 1.1M.
  Species gets 11–16M views when it covers AI.
- **Sleep and TV-length documentaries are crowded for history and space.** For the future, they are almost empty.

## 3. The niche: **"History of the Future"**

> A faceless documentary channel that explains the next 10 to 100 years (AI, work, cities, energy, space,
> money, health) through the proven parts above. Its signature feature is one no competitor can copy:
> **the Prediction Ledger.**

### What makes it unique: the Prediction Ledger
Every video records **dated, sourced forecasts**: what a named report, company or expert said would happen,
and by when. Never your own made-up numbers. Every January, a tentpole video **grades last year's ledger in
public** ("The 2026 Predictions, Graded").
- **It compounds.** By 2030 you have four years of receipts, and by 2036 ten. Nobody starting later can fake
  having tracked forecasts since 2026. **The archive is the moat.**
- **It is the opposite of inauthentic content.** It is original research and editorial judgment that you can
  check. That is exactly what YouTube's policy rewards.
- **It creates yearly return visits.** Viewers come back to see what came true.

### How each proven part maps onto it
| Proven part | How "History of the Future" uses it |
|---|---|
| Huge scale | *Every Year From Now Until 2100* · *The Next 10 Years of AI, Year by Year* |
| TV marathons | *3 Hours of the Future to Fall Asleep To*: recut flagships plus new connecting narration |
| AI with stakes | AI is one pillar of five, so the channel isn't a one-topic AI channel |
| One mystery | *What Happened to [failed 2010s future tech]?*: real, solvable stories |
| Franchises | *Cities of 2050: Part 1 / 2 / 3* · *Megaprojects Finishing Before 2040* (series) |
| "Unseen / unreal" | *Earth in 2050 Is Already Being Built*: real megaprojects shown in progress |
| Biographies of power | *The People Building 2040* (founders, labs, governments), based only on public records |
| Signature style | **One consistent illustrated look** (a color palette, a "timeline ribbon" motif, a year stamp on screen). Decide this before video 1 |

### The five content pillars
1. **AI and work**: jobs, tools, the economy
2. **Cities and megaprojects**: what is physically being built
3. **Energy and climate**: the infrastructure version, not the opinion version
4. **Space**: missions with real launch dates
5. **Money and power**: the companies and countries shaping 2030–2050

## 5. The 10-year plan

| Period | Goal | Output | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nov 2026 – Jan 2027: Build** | Lock the style, voice, Ledger system and 6 banked scripts | Art style guide, `voice.md`, a Ledger spreadsheet (claim, source, date, deadline) | 6 scripts ready before upload #1 |
| **2027: Find the formula** | Discover which pillar and which length gets outliers | 1 flagship every 2 weeks (~24), 3 Shorts a week, 2 marathons | Run `/yt-retention` on every video. Double down on any 3×+ outlier with a Part 2 within 30 days |
| **2028: Franchise** | Turn the winners into named series | Cut down to the 2 best pillars, monthly series episodes, marathons every 6 weeks | A series with 3+ episodes, each above the channel median |
| **2029: Multiply** | Reach more languages and the TV screen | Multi-language audio tracks on the top 20 videos, 3-hour TV editions | Over 40% of watch time from TV (check in Studio) |
| **2030: Halftime tentpole** | **"We Tracked the Future From 2026 to 2030"**, the channel's biggest video | A 60–90 minute documentary built from four years of the Ledger | This is the video only this channel can make |
| **2031–2033: Expand** | Spin-offs, using the best pillar as a second channel | e.g. a separate *Future of Money* channel, a yearly Ledger report as a free PDF or email list | The second channel launches with a back catalogue and an audience |
| **2034–2036: The archive** | **"10 Years of Predicting the Future: Graded"** | The decade finale, and a new 10-year Ledger opened for 2036–2046 | The channel's history *is* the content. Restart the cycle |

### Weekly rhythm (from `/yt-plan`'s "one anchor, one cheap one, Shorts from the anchor")
- **One anchor** every 2 weeks: a researched flagship
- **One cheap one** in the off week: a Ledger update, a follow-up to the most-asked comment (`/yt-comment`), or a marathon recut
- **Three Shorts** each week, cut from the anchor. Each one is a single dated prediction with its source on screen

## 6. Rules that keep it monetized and believable
1. **Original script every time**, with sources listed in the description. No invented numbers. When a forecast
   isn't sourced, don't put it in.
2. **One consistent voice** (yours, or one licensed voice used every time) and **original visuals**. No templated stock slideshows.
3. **Disclose realistic AI-generated visuals** using YouTube's altered or synthetic content label when they could pass as real footage.
4. **No borrowed brands** (film characters, sports leagues, celebrities as thumbnails).
5. **Grade yourself honestly in the Ledger.** Being visibly wrong is what makes the channel trustworthy.

---

## 7. Two alternatives, if "History of the Future" doesn't suit you
- **"Unseen Earth, 2 Hours"**: TV-length, calm documentaries about real places, using Travpedia's and Lifeder's proven parts. It's lower risk but more crowded, and it has no built-in moat.
- **Animated character history**: following OverSimplified's 39.5M median. It has the highest ceiling, but it needs real animation skill or budget and a comic voice. It's the hardest to do faceless at quality.
